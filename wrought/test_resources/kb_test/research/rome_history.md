The history of Rome spans over a thousand years, from its legendary founding in
753 BCE to the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 CE. It began as a small
city-state on the Tiber River, ruled by kings, before transitioning into the
Roman Republic in 509 BCE. The Republic was defined by a system of elected
officials, a Senate, and a focus on military expansion. Rome’s conquests brought
wealth but also internal strife, leading to civil wars and the rise of powerful
generals like Julius Caesar.

Caesar’s assassination in 44 BCE paved the way for the end of the Republic and
the rise of the Roman Empire under Augustus in 27 BCE. The Empire saw
unprecedented stability and expansion, covering much of Europe, North Africa,
and the Middle East. The Pax Romana (27 BCE–180 CE) marked a period of relative
peace and prosperity. However, economic troubles, internal corruption, and
external invasions weakened Rome. The empire split into eastern and western
halves in 285 CE, and in 476 CE, the Western Roman Empire collapsed under
barbarian pressures, while the Eastern Roman Empire (Byzantium) endured for
another thousand years.
