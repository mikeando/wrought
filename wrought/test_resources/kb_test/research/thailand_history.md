Thailand’s history traces back to early Tai-speaking peoples who migrated from
southern China into mainland Southeast Asia. By the 13th century, the Kingdom of
Sukhothai (founded in 1238) emerged as one of the first recognized Thai states,
laying the foundation for Thai culture, script, and governance. It was soon
eclipsed by the Ayutthaya Kingdom (1350–1767), which became a dominant regional
power through trade, military expansion, and diplomacy with China, Persia, and
European powers.

In 1767, Burmese forces sacked Ayutthaya, but the Thai general Taksin regrouped
and established a new capital in Thonburi. His successor, King Rama I, founded
the Chakri Dynasty in 1782 and moved the capital to Bangkok, where the monarchy
remains today. Thailand maintained its independence during the colonial era,
skillfully navigating European pressures while modernizing under King
Chulalongkorn (Rama V) in the late 19th century.

In the 20th century, Thailand transitioned from an absolute to a constitutional
monarchy in 1932. It experienced military coups, political turmoil, and economic
growth, while maintaining its unique status as the only Southeast Asian nation
never colonized. Today, Thailand remains a key regional power, balancing
tradition with modern development.
